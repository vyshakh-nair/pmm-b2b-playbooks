# Discovery-Focused Battlecard

For early calls, before the prospect has settled on a shortlist. This isn't a pitch. It's finding out whether you're even the right fit, and quietly ruling competitors in or out along the way.

**Product:** *[Your Product]*

## 1. Questions That Surface Real Pain

Open-ended. If a question can be answered yes or no, rewrite it. AVOID. USING. JARGON. Keep it simple.

- "Walk me through what happens today when [problem event] occurs, start to finish." -> Understand the problem.
- "Who else touches this process, and where does it usually get stuck?" -> Who are the primary users and what are the bottlenecks?
- "What have you already tried? Why didn't it stick?" -> Find out if they have any existing internal solutions or an incumbent provider to solve the problem, if yes, ask why they couldn't solve it.
- "If this doesn't get solved in the next two quarters, what happens?" - Helps understand impact of not solving their problem.

Let them finish two or three answers before you start connecting dots out loud. Jumping straight to a feature the moment you hear a pain point reads as a pitch, not a conversation.

## 2. Questions That Surface the Competitive Field

Ask these without naming your own competitors first. See who they bring up unprompted.

- "Who else have you talked to about this?"
- "Is there an internal favorite right now?"
- "What would make you rule a vendor out early?"

## 3. Reading the Answers

| If they say... | It probably means... | Competitor likely in play |
|---|---|---|
| "We need something that just works out of the box" | Low tolerance for implementation time | *[Competitor known for heavy setup]* |
| "Our security team is strict" | Compliance will gate the deal | *[Competitor weak on certifications]* |
| "We've been burned by a vendor before" | Trust matters more than features right now | *[any — lead with references]* |

## 4. Early Disqualifiers

Signs this isn't a fit, worth naming in week one rather than week eight:

- *[e.g., no budget owner identified, timeline longer than 12 months, hard requirement you don't meet]*

A bad-fit deal killed early costs you an hour. Killed late, it costs a quarter.
